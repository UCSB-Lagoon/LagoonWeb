## What is a pass time?

A pass time is the specific date-and-time window when GOLD opens registration for you. Before your window opens, the register button simply doesn't work for you; once it opens, you're racing everyone whose window opened earlier. Pass times are assigned per student, per quarter — yours will not match your roommate's.

You can find yours in GOLD under the registration section — it's posted well before registration opens each quarter. Put it in your calendar with an alert. The single most common registration mistake at UCSB is not a bad plan; it's forgetting the window.

## How the three passes work

UCSB splits registration into three passes, and each pass has a unit cap — you can only register for a limited number of units per pass, with the cap rising as passes progress. Check GOLD for the current caps each quarter.

| Pass | What it's for in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Pass 1 | Your must-have classes — major requirements, anything that fills fast |
| Pass 2 | The rest of a full schedule — GEs, electives, second-choice sections |
| Pass 3 | Cleanup — swaps, waitlists, and grabbing seats that opened up |

The strategy writes itself once you see the structure: spend Pass 1 on scarcity, not preference. A popular GE you could take any quarter is a Pass 2 pick; the major course that fills in an hour is what Pass 1 exists for.

## How to prep before your window opens

Registration goes smoothly when the decisions are already made. Aim to spend your pass time clicking, not thinking.

-   Build your ideal schedule *and* a backup before your window — sections fill, and deciding under a countdown is how you end up with an 8am you hate.
-   Check enrollment counts in GOLD the day before. If a section is nearly full, promote it up your list.
-   Look at grade distributions when you're choosing between professors — real data beats dorm-floor rumors. The [grade distributions guide](/ucsb-grade-distributions-guide) covers how to use it well.
-   Know your alternates by course number so you can search them instantly.

## What if a class you need is full?

Don't panic at Pass 3. Waitlists move more than people expect during the first week of the quarter as students drop and shuffle. Show up to the first lectures of a full class if the professor allows crashing, watch GOLD for openings, and have your backup schedule actually registered in the meantime — a real schedule you can trade out of beats a perfect schedule that doesn't exist.

## Quick answers

<FaqList>

<FaqItem q="Where do I find my UCSB pass time?" open>

In GOLD, under the registration section — pass times are posted there before each quarter's registration opens. Each student's windows are assigned individually.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="What's the difference between Pass 1, Pass 2, and Pass 3?">

Each pass is a registration window with a unit cap. Use Pass 1 for must-have and fast-filling classes, Pass 2 to complete a full schedule, and Pass 3 for swaps and waitlist cleanup.

</FaqItem>

<FaqItem q="What should I do if a class is full at my pass time?">

Register a backup so you hold a real schedule, join the waitlist if there is one, and watch the first week of the quarter — seats open as students drop and shuffle.

</FaqItem>
