# Lagoon UC Expansion Research
**Date:** March 30, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Helena (Enrich Labs AI Marketing Agent)  
**Purpose:** Identify and rank the top UC campus expansion targets beyond UCSB

---

## Overview

Lagoon currently serves UCSB's ~37,000 undergraduates as a campus life super-app aggregating class schedules (via GOLD), live dining menus, campus events, grade distributions, student government data, and Daily Nexus news. This report evaluates the other 9 UC campuses as expansion targets across three dimensions: student population size, quality of existing campus apps, and tech-savviness indicators.

**UCSF is excluded** from this analysis. It is a graduate/professional health sciences campus with approximately 3,200 students — no relevant undergraduate population and no meaningful overlap with Lagoon's product.

---

## Campus-by-Campus Profiles

### 1. UCLA
- **Total enrollment (Fall 2025):** 47,435 — the largest UC campus and one of the largest public universities in the US
- **Existing apps:**
  - *BruinDining* (student-built, App Store) — dining-only, no schedule integration
  - *ASUCLA app* — hours and events for restaurants and the student union only
  - *Transact Mobile Ordering* — dining payment/ordering, requires active meal plan
  - No unified campus life super-app exists
- **Tech-savviness indicators:** Strong CS and engineering programs; Los Angeles startup ecosystem (adjacent to tech companies in Santa Monica, Culver City, Playa Vista); active student developer community evidenced by the student-built BruinDining app; Daily Bruin is one of the most-read college newspapers in the country

---

### 2. UC Berkeley
- **Total enrollment (Fall 2025):** 46,151
- **Existing apps:**
  - *Berkeley Mobile* (ASUC Office of the Chief Technology Officer, OCTO) — includes dining menus, library hours, gym info, campus resources; rated 4.1 stars but only 14 ratings on the App Store (very low adoption)
  - *Grubhub* officially integrated with Berkeley dining meal plans
  - A relatively mature student-government-built app already exists
- **Tech-savviness indicators:** Ranked #1 in data science and #2 in computer science nationally by US News 2026; home to the Berkeley Skydeck startup accelerator; extremely active open-source and hackathon culture; proximity to Silicon Valley

### 3. UC San Diego

- **Existing apps:**

  - *Triton2Go* — mobile ordering and reusable container program for dining, not a super-app
  - No unified campus life super-app with schedule + dining + events
- **Tech-savviness indicators:** Jacobs School of Engineering has 4 undergraduate programs ranked in the national Top 10 (including Computer Systems ranked #9); one of the fastest-growing tech research campuses in the US; home to Qualcomm Institute and UCSD Halıcıoğlu Data Science Institute; strong engineering/STEM-dominant student body

### 4. UC Davis

- **Tech-savviness indicators:** Strong computer science and engineering programs; large agricultural tech and biotech research presence; active hackathon community (AggieHacks)

### 5. UC Irvine
- **Total enrollment:** ~36,000

  - Dining information lives primarily on the UCI website

### 6. UC Riverside
- **Total enrollment (Fall 2025):** ~28,000+ (record-breaking growth; the largest year-over-year increase in new student enrollment of any UC campus in Fall 2025, with 8,297 new students matriculating)
- **Existing apps:**
  - No prominent student super-app or unified campus life app found
  - Dining information is web-only
- **Tech-savviness indicators:** Growing CS and engineering programs; university has invested significantly in infrastructure to support the enrollment surge; 96% California residents (very high in-state engagement); underserved population with high first-generation student representation (46%)

### 7. UC Santa Cruz
- **Total enrollment:** ~19,500
- **Existing apps:**
  - *UCSC Dining Menu* (third-party, student-built) — rated 4.6/5 with 304 ratings; covers 5 dining halls for 10 colleges, real-time menus — high adoption for a single-use app
  - *SlugDining* (student-built) — rated 4.4/5 with 14 ratings; launched more recently
  - *GET app* — dining payment management only
  - Multiple fragmented apps signal strong student demand for a unified solution with no one filling that gap
- **Tech-savviness indicators:** UC Santa Cruz has produced significant contributions to open-source computing (the original UCSC Genome Browser, influence on Linux); Silicon Valley proximity drives strong tech culture; 10-college system creates a unique need for campus-wide aggregation; culturally close to UCSB (coastal college town, strong student identity)

## Top 3 Expansion Targets

---

### #1 — UC San Diego (Score: 15/15)

**Why UCSD is the strongest target:**

UC San Diego is effectively the UCSB of Southern California — a coastal, research-heavy, STEM-dominant campus with an extremely active student body and no meaningful campus super-app. The official UC San Diego app holds a 3.2/5 rating on the App Store with just 58 reviews despite 10k+ downloads, indicating a large and dissatisfied user base actively looking for better tools. The Triton2Go app covers dining ordering but nothing else.

UCSD's structure maps closely to UCSB's: both have multiple dining facilities, a college-based residential system (UCSD has 8 colleges vs. UCSB's residential colleges), a comparable academic portal (WebReg vs. GOLD), and an active campus newspaper (The UCSD Guardian). This structural similarity means Lagoon's existing integrations and feature logic would require the least re-engineering of any campus.

With a current enrollment of 45,087 — and a Board of Regents-approved plan to expand to 56,000 by 2040 — UCSD represents both Lagoon's largest immediate addressable audience and its fastest-growing long-term opportunity. The Jacobs School of Engineering produces students who are natural early adopters and potential on-campus ambassadors.

**Recommended go-to-market approach:** Target Sixth College and Muir College students first (both STEM-heavy, active dining halls), partner with The UCSD Guardian for co-promotion, and pitch the app during Welcome Week orientation.

---

### #2 — UC Santa Cruz (Score: 14/15)

**Why UCSC is the second-best target:**

UC Santa Cruz punches well above its enrollment weight for this analysis. Its 304-review UCSC Dining Menu app (rated 4.6 stars) is the clearest demand signal in the entire UC system for what Lagoon already does — a campus-built, student-loved food and campus info tool. That app exists because UCSC students needed one, and they built it themselves. But it covers only dining. There is no class schedule integration, no events aggregation, no grade distributions, no student government data. The gap between what students have and what Lagoon offers is exactly the gap UCSC students have been trying to fill piecemeal.

UCSC's 10-college structure also creates a platform network effect opportunity: students identify strongly with their individual college (Cowell, Porter, Crown, etc.) in much the same way UCSB students identify with Isla Vista, making hyper-local features (per-dining-hall menus, college-specific events) highly valued.

The campus's Silicon Valley proximity and open-source heritage (UCSC is literally where the UCSC Genome Browser and key Linux contributions originated) means tech adoption curves are steep. A student-built, locally branded app will get organic word-of-mouth in this community.

**Recommended go-to-market approach:** Reach out directly to the student developer behind the UCSC Dining Menu app as a potential campus partner or advisor. Focus launch timing on Fall Week 1 when students are navigating 10 new dining halls simultaneously.

---

### #3 — UCLA (Score: 12/15)

**Why UCLA is the third target:**

UCLA is the largest UC campus by enrollment (47,435 students) and the most recognizable UC brand. The existence of the student-built BruinDining app proves demand for exactly what Lagoon offers — but BruinDining covers only dining. There is no integrated schedule, grade distribution, events, or student government layer. The ASUCLA app covers restaurant hours for food vendors, and Transact handles meal ordering. None of these communicate with each other. The market is genuinely fragmented.

UCLA's tech-savviness is very high. The Los Angeles tech and entertainment industry creates a student body that skews toward product-minded, early-adopter behavior. UCLA's proximity to tech companies in the Westside corridor and its strong CS program mean there are students willing to build on top of Lagoon (possible future API/developer community angle).

The one reason UCLA ranks third rather than first is cultural fit and complexity. UCLA is a significantly more urban, commuter-heavy campus than UCSB or UCSD, which dilutes some of the "campus life consolidation" value proposition — commuter students care less about dining hall menus and more about class schedules and events. The administrative complexity of a 47k-student institution is also greater. That said, the raw TAM makes it too large to ignore as a third target.

**Recommended go-to-market approach:** Focus initial launch on residential students in the Hill (the main housing cluster) where dining, events, and campus life features are most relevant. Partner with Daily Bruin for editorial coverage. Engage the BruinDining developer community as potential early contributors.

## Campuses to Deprioritize

**UC Berkeley:** Berkeley Mobile (ASUC OCTO) already covers the super-app territory with institutional backing. Competing requires differentiating against a government-funded student org, which is a harder positioning challenge than competing with a neglected official app.

**UC Davis:** The UC Davis Mobile app is the most comprehensive official app in the UC system, covering transit, email, schedule, newspaper, and news in a single interface. The market gap Lagoon fills simply does not exist at Davis to the same degree.

**UC Riverside:** Rapid growth and zero existing app infrastructure makes UCR appealing on paper, but the campus skews heavily first-generation and lower-income (46% first-gen vs. ~30% at top UC campuses). This demographic tends toward high utility/low friction tools, and the university's primary tech investments are in infrastructure rather than developer culture. Revisit in 2–3 years once enrollment stabilizes above 30,000.

**UC Merced:** Too small (10,000 students) to justify expansion investment at Lagoon's current early-access stage. Revisit when enrollment reaches 15,000+.

## Key Strategic Considerations for Any Expansion

1. **Data integration per campus is the primary technical bottleneck.** Each campus uses a different academic portal (WebReg at UCSD, CalCentral at Berkeley, StudentAccess at UCI). Lagoon will need campus-specific API work or scraping logic for each new target.

2. **Brand localization matters.** "Built for Gauchos, by Gauchos" only works at UCSB. Each new campus should get a version of Lagoon that feels native — local mascot references, campus color accents, campus-specific branding for each dining hall.

3. **Student ambassador programs scale better than paid ads at this stage.** Each campus has student developers, student government reps, and RA networks that can be recruited as ground-level evangelists. The BruinDining and SlugDining developers are potential early-campus leads.

4. **Grade distribution data is a viral feature.** At UCSB, grade distributions drive significant organic word-of-mouth. UCSD's grade data is publicly available through its academic systems. Confirming data availability early in the expansion evaluation reduces risk.
